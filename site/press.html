150 words

RabbitSoftware is an independent project that builds research software people can inspect, question and control. Its hosted workspace, RabbitOS, is open without an account: it shows public research records with their original source links (PubMed, Europe PMC, ClinicalTrials.gov, ClinVar and others), an open data hub, a brain and body explorer, and AI chat where users approve what is sent. Personal data stays on the user’s own device. Changes to the open research data are written to a public, hash-linked ledger that is copied to several places, mirrored by a small proof-of-work chain, and timestamped through Bitcoin using OpenTimestamps, so anyone can check that nothing was quietly altered. The ledger is a tamper-evident log, not a cryptocurrency, and a timestamp shows when something existed, not that it is true. RabbitSoftware is research tooling, not clinical software. It is created and maintained by Chase Allen Ringquist and supported by voluntary donations. rabbitsoftware.io

Key messages

  • Check the work. Every answer keeps its source. Every change leaves a record anyone can verify.
  • Your data stays yours. Personal data stays on your device; the public chain only holds fingerprints and public records.
  • Honest about limits. Research prototypes are labeled as such. AI summaries are labeled unverified. Timestamps show when, not whether it is true.
  • Checkable. The status page, the ledger, the Maxwell chain and the Bitcoin timestamps are public, so claims can be tested rather than trusted.

What we do and do not claim

We sayTamper-evident, publicly checkable, timestamped through Bitcoin, local-first, research tooling.
We do not sayDecentralized, unhackable, immutable, guaranteed, a cure or a diagnosis, an investment, a token or coin, or that AI output is verified.
If asked about healthRabbitSoftware does not diagnose, recommend treatment or edit DNA in a body. It is for exploring public research.
If asked about moneyThere is no token or sale. Donations are voluntary gifts to running costs.

How to verify what we say
